
EXPERIMENT 1 - Same direction, different length
  A = [3, 4], B = [6, 8] (B is A doubled)
  cosine similarity  = 1.0000   <- identical direction: 'the same meaning'
  euclidean distance = 5.0000   <- far apart, only because B is bigger
  Real-life version: a 2-page and a 20-page policy about the SAME topic produce vectors of different length.
  Cosine ignores length and compares topic. That is why it is the usual choice for text.

EXPERIMENT 2 - Rank sentences for the query: 'Question about our loans and customer service'
  sentence                                                              cosine  distance
  Annual report on lending volumes and servicing (very long document)    0.998     5.916
  Customer asks about home loan eligibility                              0.987     2.236
  Branch opening hours and holiday notice                                0.898     6.633
  Mortgage application is missing KYC documents                          0.730     5.385
  Verify identity documents for account opening                          0.525     6.856
  Customer complains about a delayed card chargeback                     0.327     8.124
  Suspicious transaction flagged as possible fraud                       0.162     8.832
  Best by cosine   : Annual report on lending volumes and servicing (very long document)
  Best by distance : Customer asks about home loan eligibility
  THEY DISAGREE. Cosine prefers the very long document: it points the same way as the query.
  Distance prefers the short, on-topic sentence: the long document's vector is simply far away (it is big).
  Neither is always right. It is one reason retrieval systems cut documents into chunks of similar size (Day 4).

EXPERIMENT 3 - Shrink every vector to length 1 and the two measures become the same story
  cosine(loan, mortgage)                       = 0.9718
  dot product of the unit-length versions      = 0.9718   (same number)
  distance^2 of unit-length versions           = 0.0563
  2 - 2 x cosine                               = 0.0563   (same number)
  So on unit-length vectors: higher cosine <=> smaller distance. Many embedding models output unit-length vectors.

EXPERIMENT 4 - Do professional libraries give the same answers?
  cosine    mine=0.971825  numpy=0.971825
